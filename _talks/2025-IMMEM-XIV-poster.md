Background: *Achromobacter* spp. are emerging opportunistic pathogens in individuals with chronic respiratory diseases such as cystic fibrosis (CF). These bacteria are associated with chronic infections, antimicrobial resistance, and poor clinical outcomes. Among them, a particularly concerning lineage—the Danish epidemic strain (DES)—has transmitted among patients in Denmark. DES is multidrug-resistant and adapted to the CF airway, yet it remains unclear which genetic determinants led to this phenotype, when it emerged, and whether this lineage is restricted to Denmark or has spread globally.

Methods: We analysed genomes of a set of 60  longitudinally sampled  *A. ruhlandii* isolates from 19 people with CF (pwCF) (of which 58 belonged to DES) attending the CF center at Rigshospitalet. PwCF were sampled over a period of up to 21 years, including long read sequencing of two representative DES isolates to reconstruct their complete genomes. After the addition of genomes from 79 isolates from public databases, the final dataset consisted of 139 *A. ruhlandii* genomes from 13 countries. We used comparative genomics to identify lineage-specific genetic features of DES and constructed a core genome phylogeny to contextualise DES among global isolates and to estimate the timeline of DES emergence and transmission among patients. 

Results: Phylogenetic analysis confirmed that DES forms a monophyletic clade within *A. ruhlandii* and likely emerged around 1990. Despite extensive screening of publicly available genomes, we found no evidence of DES or closely related lineages outside Denmark. Comparative genomic analyses identified multiple lineage-specific features, including the chromosomal mutations and acquisition of large mobile genetic elements, that may contribute to the phenotype of DES.

Conclusions: DES is a highly adapted and resistant lineage of *A. ruhlandii* that appears to be geographically restricted to Denmark. Its distinct genomic profile provides insight into the mechanisms underlying bacterial adaptation to human hosts and helps elucidate the genetic determinants that lead to success in establishing chronic infections and epidemic transmission. Ultimately, these findings emphasise the need for continued surveillance and genomic investigation of *Achromobacter* spp. in chronic bacterial infections especially in pwCF worldwide.
