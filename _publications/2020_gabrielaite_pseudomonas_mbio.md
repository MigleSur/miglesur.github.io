*Pseudomonas aeruginosa* causes chronic lung infections in cystic fibrosis patients, where it undergoes long-term within-host evolution. Using longitudinal whole genome sequencing of clinical isolates, we show that adaptation is driven not only by point mutations but also by extensive gene loss and horizontal gene acquisition. Many of the lost genes are associated with acute virulence, while acquired genes frequently relate to metabolism and niche adaptation. These results highlight gene content variation as a major evolutionary mechanism during chronic infection and emphasize the importance of pan-genome dynamics in bacterial pathogenesis.
